How to choose an app blocker for Android (and what to avoid)

Updated November 25, 2026 · 6 minute read

Google Play has dozens of apps that block apps and cut screen time. Almost all of them promise the same thing, but they do different things. This guide gives you a checklist to pick the one that solves your problem and to avoid the ones that ask for more than they need.

First, define what you want to fix

  • You spend too much time overall. You need daily limits per app and stats.
  • One section hooks you (Reels, Shorts, Explore). You need section blocking, not just app blocking.
  • You get distracted at certain times (studying, working, sleeping). You need schedules, or rules by place and Wi-Fi.
  • You remove the block every time you are tempted. You need protection that is hard to switch off.

A checklist to compare apps

CriterionWhat to check
Per-app limitsCan you set a different daily time for each app?
Section blockingDoes it block just Reels or Shorts, or does it force you to block the whole app?
Schedules and placesCan you pick days and hours, or rules by place or Wi-Fi network?
ProtectionIs there a password, an emergency unlock or a mode that is deliberately hard to turn off?
Price and adsWhich features are behind a payment? Does it show ads, which work against the goal of being less distracted?
AccountCan you use it without creating an account? The less data you hand over, the better.
PrivacyWhat does it read, where is it processed and is it sent to a server? Check the Google Play listing and its privacy policy.
MaintenanceWas it updated recently? Section-blocking apps depend on keeping up with changes in Instagram and YouTube.

Permissions: what is normal and what is not

To work, a blocker needs to know which app you have open. Android allows that through permissions such as usage access, drawing over other apps or the accessibility service, which can also recognize sections inside an app. Asking for them is normal. Asking for your contacts, SMS or location without explaining why is not. Before you accept the accessibility service, check that the app clearly explains what it uses it for and what it does with what it reads.

How to test an app before committing

  1. Install only one app at a time, so you know which one works.
  2. Create a single rule for your biggest problem (for example, Reels or 30 minutes of one social network).
  3. Use it for three days without changing anything and see if your screen time drops.
  4. If it gets in the way more than it helps, uninstall it. You do not have to keep it.

Frequently asked questions

Does a blocker work if I can just uninstall it?

You can uninstall any app, so the value is in what makes it harder in a moment of impulse: a password, a pause before an emergency unlock, or a mode such as nuclear mode whose early exit takes a long wait.

Do I have to pay to block apps?

Not necessarily. Some apps charge for their main features, and others, like Momentum, offer all their features for free.

Is it safe to give an app the accessibility permission?

It is a powerful permission, so only give it to an app that explains what it uses it for and whose listing and privacy policy say what it does with the information. You can turn it off at any time in Settings > Accessibility.
