Digital detox: a 7-day plan to start today

Updated November 25, 2026 · 6 minute read

A digital detox does not mean throwing your phone away. It means deciding when and why you use it. This plan spreads the changes over a week so you do not give up on day two.

Day 1: measure

Do not change anything yet. Write down how much time you spend today overall and in your three most used apps. That number is your starting point.

Day 2: silence

Turn off notifications for everything that is not a person. Without alerts, the phone stops calling you.

Day 3: set limits

Pick two apps and give each a realistic daily limit. In Momentum you assign a time to each app or social network and it locks when you reach the cap.

Day 4: block the section that hooks you

Turn on the block for Reels, Shorts or Explore, whichever is yours. You can still use messages. See how in the guide to blocking Reels and Shorts.

Day 5: protect your sleep

Schedule a night block when distracting apps are unavailable, and charge your phone outside the bed.

Day 6: simplify the screen

Try a minimalist launcher for a day: only phone, messages, contacts and the apps you allow. You will notice how often you reach for an icon out of reflex.

Day 7: review and decide

Compare with day 1. See which rules helped and which got in the way, and keep the ones that worked. The plan does not end here: it is a starting point for more intentional use.

If limits are hard to keep, protect your rules with a password. Keep nuclear mode, which locks apps for 1 to 3 months, for when you know what works for you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to quit social media?

No. The goal is to use it by decision, not by reflex. Many people keep their networks with limits and without the sections that hook them most.

How long does a digital detox last?

It can be a day, a week or a month. To start, seven days is enough to see what changes.
