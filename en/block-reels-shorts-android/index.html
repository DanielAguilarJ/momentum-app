How to block Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts on Android

Updated November 25, 2026 · 6 minute read

You open Instagram "for a second" to check a message and forty minutes later you are still swiping short videos. This guide covers what you can do with the settings your phone and the apps already have, and how to block just that section with Momentum.

Why short videos are so hard to quit

Reels, Shorts and TikTok videos share one design: each clip lasts seconds, the next one starts by itself and there is no natural ending. Nothing on the screen tells you that you have seen enough. That is why willpower often loses to design, and why it helps to put up a barrier that does not depend on you remembering in the moment.

What you can do without installing anything

On YouTube: limit or turn off Shorts

YouTube added a daily timer for the Shorts feed in October 2025 and, in April 2026, began letting you set it to zero minutes, which turns off most Shorts on your phone. You find it in the app's time management setting. Where it sits and whether you have it depends on your YouTube version and account, so update the app if you do not see it. With a limit above zero, the prompt that appears when you reach it can be dismissed, so it works as a soft brake.

On Instagram: there is no switch for Reels

Instagram has no setting that turns Reels off. You can reduce them: switch your home feed to Following, tap "Not interested" on Reels you do not want and adjust your suggested content preferences. It helps, but Reels still show up in Explore and in messages.

On Android: a limit for the whole app

The app timers in Android's Digital Wellbeing limit the entire app. They work, but when time runs out they also block direct messages, which you may actually need.

Block just the section with Momentum

Momentum blocks the specific section (Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Explore and others) and leaves the rest of the app available. It also has safeguards so you do not turn the rule off in a moment of temptation.

  1. Install Momentum from Google Play. You do not need to create an account.
  2. Open Settings > In-app blocking. You will see ready-made rules such as Instagram Reels, Explore, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels, among others.
  3. Tap the notice to turn on monitoring. Momentum explains what it uses the accessibility service for and takes you to Android settings to switch it on.
  4. Turn on the rules you want. The screen shows how many are active.
  5. Open Instagram or YouTube. When you enter the blocked section, Momentum shows its block screen or sends you back to the home screen, and the rest of the app keeps working.

Stop yourself from switching it off

  • Password: protect your rules so that changing them requires a password.
  • Communication-only mode: allows messages and blocks feeds, stories and reels.
  • Schedules: block by days and hours, for example while you study or sleep.
  • Nuclear mode: the strongest block. Once it is on, it cannot be removed until the period you chose ends. Use it with care.

An honest limitation

Apps change their design often. If Instagram or YouTube change how a section is built, detection can fail until Momentum is updated, so keep the app up to date.

Frequently asked questions

Does blocking Reels also block Instagram messages?

No. The Reels rule blocks only that section. You can keep using messages and the rest of the app.

Does it work with TikTok?

Momentum includes rules for several apps, including TikTok, Facebook, Snapchat and X, as well as Instagram and YouTube. The sections available vary by app.

Does Momentum read my messages?

It does not store or send to any server what the accessibility service reads. That information is checked on your phone in the moment and is not shared with third parties.

Does it cost anything?

No. All features are free and the app has no ads.
