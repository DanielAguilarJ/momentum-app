How to stop doomscrolling: 7 tactics that work

Updated November 25, 2026 · 5 minute read

Doomscrolling is swiping endlessly through news or posts, often bad ones, even when it is making you feel worse. It is not a lack of character: the apps are designed so there is no stopping point. The fix is to add friction to the path.

1. Notice when it happens

For three days, note the moments you open the app without deciding to: on waking, in bed, in the bathroom, when bored, after an argument. There is almost always a trigger, and it is easier to change one moment than a whole habit.

2. Take the app out of the way

Move the app off your home screen into a folder on the last page. Each extra step is a chance to reconsider.

3. Block the section that hooks you

The endless scroll usually lives in one place: Reels, Shorts, Explore or "For you". If you block only that section, you keep messages and everything else. There is a step-by-step explanation in the guide to blocking Reels and Shorts.

4. Set a daily cap

Pick a realistic time for each app, say 30 minutes, and let the app lock when you reach it. Deciding once is easier than deciding every time you open your phone.

5. Protect the night

Late-night doomscrolling steals sleep. Schedule a block for the hour before bed and charge your phone away from the bed, so it is not the first thing you touch on waking.

6. Keep a replacement within reach

If the gap stays empty, the scrolling returns. Leave a book, a notebook or headphones where the phone used to be. For work or study, a timed focus session helps you start. See the Pomodoro technique guide.

7. Silence what calls you

Turn off notifications for news and for social suggestions. Keep only the ones from people.

If phone use keeps you from sleeping, affects your mood or constantly interferes with your work or relationships, talk to a health professional. An app can help with habits, but it does not replace that support.

Frequently asked questions

Is it the same as phone addiction?

Not necessarily. Doomscrolling is a specific pattern: consuming content nonstop, usually negative. It can be part of problematic phone use, but it is not a diagnosis.

Is blocking the whole app better?

Sometimes, for example during sleeping hours. But if you need the app for something else, it is more sustainable to block only the section that hooks you.
