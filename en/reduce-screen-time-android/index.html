How to reduce screen time on your phone: 8 steps that work

Updated November 25, 2026 · 6 minute read

Saying "I will use my phone less" rarely works. What works is changing your surroundings so that using it less becomes the easy option. Here are eight steps, in order, that you can apply today on Android.

1. Measure how much you use each app first

Before setting goals, look at the real numbers. Android has its Digital Wellbeing dashboard, and Momentum shows how long you spend in each app, your most used apps, your progress week by week and a weekly summary compared with the week before. Most people are surprised by the result.

2. Pick only two or three culprit apps

Trying to control everything at once is a recipe for giving up on day three. Look at your list and choose the two or three apps where time disappears without you noticing, usually one social network and one video app.

3. Set a daily limit for those apps

A daily limit turns "just a bit more" into a concrete decision. In Momentum you assign a time to each app or social network and, when you reach it, the app is blocked.

4. Block the section, not the whole app

Often the problem is not the app but one part of it: Reels, Shorts or the Explore tab. If you block the whole app you lose messages too, and that pushes you to remove the block. Blocking just the section that hooks you is easier to keep. The steps are in the guide to blocking Reels and Shorts.

5. Schedule hours for sleep and for work

Late-night scrolling is one of the hardest habits to cut. A scheduled block, with the days and hours you choose, makes distracting apps unavailable exactly when they tempt you most: while studying, working or before bed. Momentum also supports rules by place or Wi-Fi network.

6. Remove notifications you do not need

Many times you pick up the phone because a notification called you. In Android settings, turn off notifications from apps that are not people: promotions, video suggestions, "we miss you". Keep messages and calls.

7. Make your phone more boring

Colors and icons are designed to grab attention. Two changes help: move tempting apps off your home screen and, on many phones, switch the screen to grayscale during rest hours. To go further, a minimalist launcher leaves only phone, messages and contacts.

8. Replace the habit, do not just remove it

If you remove scrolling and put nothing in its place, the gap fills with another app. Use that time for a focus session: Momentum has a Pomodoro timer (25/5) or your own duration, with daily and weekly goals and streaks. Check your summary every week and adjust your limits.

A useful warning: Momentum's strictest mode (nuclear mode) locks the apps you choose for 1 to 3 months, and turning it off early takes a long, uninterrupted wait with the app open. Start with normal limits protected by a password, and keep nuclear mode for when you already know your habits.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours of screen time are too many?

There is no number that fits everyone. What helps is comparing with your own goal: how much time you would like to give each app and how much you actually give it. That is why the first step is to measure.

Do I have to pay to use time limits?

No. In Momentum the limits, schedules and the other features are available without payment.

Can I keep using my messages?

Yes. You can block only the sections that distract you or use communication-only mode, which allows messages and blocks feeds, stories and reels.
