Your life in weeks: what it is and how to see it on your phone

Updated November 25, 2026 · 4 minute read

An 80-year life fits in a grid of 4,160 little squares. Each one is a week. Seeing all of them on a single screen changes how you think about time.

Where the idea comes from

The visualization became popular through the article "Your Life in Weeks", which writer Tim Urban published in 2014 on Wait But Why. Urban draws a 90-year life (4,680 weeks) as a grid where each row is a year, and asks whether you are making the most of your weeks. The idea connects to a much older tradition, memento mori: remembering that time is finite so you can decide better how to use it.

How it is calculated

A year has 52 weeks, so 80 years is 80 × 52 = 4,160 weeks. Here is an example for a 25-year-old: weeks lived are in color and the ones ahead are gray.

Example illustration: 1,300 weeks lived out of 4,160. It is not a prediction for anyone; life expectancy differs from person to person.

What it is good for

  • Perspective. Seeing the total helps separate the urgent from the important.
  • Planning by weeks. A week is a more manageable unit than a year for starting a habit.
  • Less wasted time. Thinking in weeks instead of minutes makes the cost of an hour of scrolling more visible.

See it on your phone with Momentum

Momentum has a section called "My life in weeks":

  1. Enter your date of birth. The app works out your weeks lived and the ones left, based on a life expectancy that you can adjust.
  2. Pick the colors for lived weeks, future weeks and the background.
  3. Add the widget to your home screen: long-press the screen, choose "Widgets" and look for "My life in weeks".
  4. If you prefer, save the grid as an image or set it as your wallpaper.

There are also widgets for year progress and motivational quotes.

Frequently asked questions

Why 4,160 squares?

Because Momentum starts from 80 years of life: 80 times 52 weeks. You can adjust the life expectancy in the app so it fits your own circumstances better.

Is it a prediction of how long I will live?

No. It is a way to visualize time, not a forecast. No grid can tell you how long you will live.

Do I need an account?

Not to use Momentum. The app asks for your date of birth to draw your grid.
