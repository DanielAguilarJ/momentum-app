The Pomodoro technique on Android: how to use it to study or work

Updated November 25, 2026 · 5 minute read

The Pomodoro technique splits work into short blocks with breaks. It is simple, and that is why it works: starting a 25-minute task costs less than starting "studying all afternoon".

What it is

Francesco Cirillo created the method in the late 1980s and named it after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. The classic version is:

  1. Pick one task.
  2. Set a 25-minute timer and work only on that.
  3. Take a 5-minute break.
  4. After four blocks, take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

How to do it well

  • One task per block. If something new comes up, write it down and keep going.
  • Phone out of sight. If you use it as the timer, make it the app that helps you block distractions, not a social network.
  • A break is a break. Stand up, drink water, look out the window. Do not open a social network: a 5-minute break turns into 40.
  • Adjust the length. If 25 minutes feels too short or too long, change it. What matters is that it stays fixed.

Frequently asked questions

How many pomodoros a day?

It depends on your task. Start with two or three and build up slowly. Consistency matters more than quantity.

What if I get interrupted?

If it is brief, note it and go back. If it is long, void that block and start a new one when you can; a half block does not count.

Can I change from 25/5 to another length?

Yes. Momentum lets you choose the length of your focus sessions.
